Pre-CAS Interview Audio Prep Guide

Glasgow Caledonian University (MSc Project Management)

Section 1: Interview Setup Checklist

Maintain direct eye contact with your camera at all times. Do not look down or read from notes. Set your smartphone to landscape mode on a fixed tripod or stand at eye level. Ensure you are in a quiet, well-lit room with zero background noise and no other person present. Turn on Do Not Disturb mode on your phone to prevent interruptions.

Question 1: Tell me about your background and why you chose this course?

I studied Political Science at Ignatius Ajuru University of Education, where I developed an eye for how systems and governance structures operate. As a Web Developer building systems with Laravel and managing builds for organizations like Renaissance Assembly, I realized the hardest part of tech isn't just writing code. It's managing shifting scope, communication gaps between stakeholders, and changing timelines. I want the MSc Project Management to bridge my technical web development background with formal frameworks in risk assessment, resource allocation, and project leadership.

Question 2: Why the UK instead of other destinations?

UK master's programs are just 1 year, whereas destinations like the US or Canada take 2 years. A 1-year program allows me to save a full year of living expenses while obtaining an internationally recognized qualification and returning to my career without losing momentum. Furthermore, UK project management frameworks align directly with global standards like APM and PMI.

Question 3: Why Glasgow Caledonian University and the Glasgow Campus?

I chose GCU because it focuses heavily on practical industry tools like MS Project and real-world simulations, rather than pure theory. Known as the University for the Common Good, GCU embeds ethics and sustainability into business decisions. This aligns with my goal to build tech projects that drive community impact and digital inclusion. I chose the main Glasgow campus on Cowcaddens Road because Glasgow is a major business hub with a very manageable cost of living for students compared to London.

Question 4: List your modules and explain two in detail.

The modules include Ethics, Sustainability & Governance; Developing Leadership for the Common Good; Project Management; Procurement Policy and Practice; Leading Responsible Change; Financial Management for Global Decision Makers; Project Management Methodologies and Practices; Strategic Project Portfolio Management; Postgraduate Research Methods; and the Masters Research Project. Two modules I'm particularly keen on are Project Management Methodologies and Practices and Leading Responsible Change. The first covers Agile, Waterfall, and hybrid frameworks, which are critical for running software development sprint cycles. The second focuses on stakeholder management and guiding teams through digital transitions smoothly, ensuring new software is actually adopted by users without friction.

Question 5: What are your career plans after graduation?

My immediate plan is to return home to Nigeria to take on roles such as IT Project Manager or Technical Delivery Lead within corporate tech firms and software agencies. Long-term, over the next 5 to 10 years, I plan to advance into Senior Portfolio Management, leading large-scale digital transformations and civic tech projects that expand access for young people in under-resourced contexts.

Question 6: How will you handle visa work rules and funding?

My tuition balance and living expenses are fully covered by my funding sources, which will be maintained in my bank account in line with UKVI guidelines. As for visa rules, I know I am strictly limited to 20 hours per week during term time. However, my degree is my top priority; any part-time work would strictly be secondary for pocket money and networking.
